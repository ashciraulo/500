# audio/engine

Generated by `python3 audio/tools/gen_engines.py` (deterministic). Mono, 48 kHz OGG q6.
Within a family (stock, sport and straight-through together) every file shares one gain,
so idle vs redline, on vs off load, and stock vs louder exhausts keep their real
relative levels; the loudest file peaks at -1 dBTP.

## How the engines are made

`engine_synth.py` models the engine rather than layering samples: a train of
combustion pulses (one per cylinder every two crank turns) with per-cylinder
strength and timing differences and firing-to-firing variation, shaped into
rounded pressure pulses at the tailpipe, rung through two exhaust pipe sections
and a silencer, plus valve-train ticks, block resonance, intake roar, belt whine
and per-engine extras (turbo whistle, air-cooled fan, tinny classic body,
overrun crackles). Lifting off makes the firing weak and ragged. The Fire 1.2
was tuned against an outside recording of a 2007 Panda's 1.2 8v (same engine
as the Pop) for its overall spectrum. Loops are rendered as exact whole engine
cycles with the filters run round the loop, so they join without a seam.

## Sets

Each folder holds `eng_<set>_onload_<rpm>` and `eng_<set>_offload_<rpm>` loops
(about 3 s, seamless) plus `_startup`, `_shutdown` and `_limiter` one-shots.
Petrol families also come as `<set>sport` and `<set>straight` (+3 dB and +6 dB,
freer silencer, more rasp, overrun pops).

## Recording your own

Hold the engine steady at each RPM point for 3-4 seconds, under load (on a
hill or a dyno) and off load (coasting in gear), from outside near the
exhaust. Trim to a seamless loop, name it `eng_<set>_onload_<rpm>`, and drop it
in. The points don't have to match the ones above. Hear the result with
`python3 audio/tools/preview_drive.py <set>`.
